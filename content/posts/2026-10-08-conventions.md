---
title: Conventions worth breaking, quietly
summary: A menu that is a sentence, a page that is a search box, and time measured as distance.
tags: design, interfaces
---
Most personal sites are the same four boxes in a different font. The
conventions exist for good reasons, so the trick is to break them gently:
keep what they are *for*, drop what they *look like*.

## The menu is a sentence

There is no navigation bar. The first line on the home page says what I do,
and the verbs are the links. Hover one and the field changes shape before
you have decided to go.

## The page is the search box

Typing anywhere searches everything: posts, events, links, pages. Press
`Escape` to put it away.

## Time is distance

Dates are written as how far away they are: *three weeks ago*, *in nine
days*. On the events page time runs up a helix: the past is drawn in ink,
the future in pencil, and the ring is now.

## When there is nothing to scroll

On a page that does not scroll, the scroll wheel turns the object instead.
Dragging empty space does the same.
